HaveStack selects its clients.

The firm holds a limited number of engagements at any time so that every system receives senior attention from the first meeting through to its fifth year in service. Four conditions apply before an engagement opens.

Organisations, not individuals

Work is contracted with registered entities: companies, institutions, regulated financial bodies and public offices. Personal projects are declined at enquiry.

Systems, not pages

The practice builds operational software that holds records, moves transactions, enforces workflow and reports on all three. Brochure sites fall outside it.

A named owner with sign off authority

One person on the client side carries the decision. That person approves scope, resolves contention between departments and signs acceptance at each stage.

A defined budget

Cost follows scope. Figures are set against a written specification and agreed before work begins. HaveStack publishes no price list and quotes nothing before the scope is known.

What HaveStack commits to in writing.

These commitments form part of the engagement contract. They are the reason the practice is able to be selective about the work it accepts.

Delivery and documentation

Written scope before build

No development starts against a verbal brief. The scope document names every module, interface and report, and changes to it are priced and signed as amendments.

Version control from the first commit

All source sits in a repository with reviewed history. The client is given read access from the first week of the build rather than at handover.

Reproducible environments

Development, staging and production are built from the same configuration, so a release behaves the same way in each of them.

Acceptance against the specification

Each stage is tested against the written scope in the presence of the named owner. Acceptance is recorded, and defects raised there are fixed at no cost.

Security and access

Least privilege by default

Every account, service and integration receives the narrowest permission that lets it do its job, and permissions are reviewed at each release.

Encryption in transit and at rest

Traffic runs over current transport security and stored data is encrypted at the storage layer. Secrets are held in a managed store, never in source.

Audit trail on record changes

Systems that hold financial, academic or citizen records log who changed what and when, and that log cannot be edited from inside the application.

Access revoked on exit

When a person leaves the engagement on either side, their access is withdrawn the same working day and the removal is recorded.

Ownership and continuity

The client owns the code

Intellectual property in commissioned work transfers to the client on final payment. HaveStack retains no licence that would limit the client's use of it.

Credentials transfer at handover

Hosting, domains, certificates, repositories and third party accounts are registered in the client's name and handed over in full.

No dependency on the vendor

Systems are built on established open technology and documented so that another competent team can take them over without rewriting them.

Continuity if the relationship ends

The service agreement sets a notice period and a defined exit process covering data export, knowledge transfer and final documentation.

Owned and governed by Amaze Consortium.

HaveStack Technologies is the software practice of Amaze Consortium. The parent group provides the corporate structure, the financial standing and the governance that institutional clients examine before they place a system with a supplier.

That backing is why a contract with HaveStack is held by an established group rather than by a project team. Procurement panels can assess the parent, its record and its obligations as part of their evaluation, and continuity of the engagement does not rest on any individual.
